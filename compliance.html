Certifications & frameworks

Audited against recognized standards.

Encryption

Encrypted everywhere

TLS 1.3 in transit and AES-256 at rest for source records, uploads, and database connections. Documents are accessed only through short-lived signed links that expire automatically.

SOC 2

SOC 2 Type II hosting

All infrastructure runs on hosting certified to SOC 2 Type II, inheriting its physical, environmental, and operational controls.

Privacy law

Privacy law worldwide

Health and genetic data are handled as special-category data under GDPR (EEA, UK, Switzerland). n1 never sells patient data or uses it for targeted advertising, and honors regional rights to access, correction, deletion, and portability. A GDPR Data Processing Agreement (DPA) is available to customers. Read more →

HIPAA

HIPAA-compliant workflows

For deployments where HIPAA applies: BAAs with every service provider and partner that handles PHI on our behalf, and healthcare organizations can enter into a BAA directly with n1.

Certifications and attestations may vary by product, plan, and deployment, and describe the scope in which they were issued. Contact us to confirm what applies to your deployment.

Security program

Controls at every layer.

The safeguards applied to every record, account, and report.

EncryptionTLS 1.3 in transit — browser traffic and database connections. AES-256 at rest for source records and uploaded files. Document access uses short-lived signed URLs that expire automatically.
Access controlAuthenticated accounts with role-based permissions. Access to patient data is limited to authorized personnel, with access and modification logs. Sign-in with Google is supported. Passwords are checked against known breach databases and stored with modern memory-hard hashing.
InfrastructureRecords are stored encrypted on AWS hosting certified to SOC 2 Type II, with the application delivered through Cloudflare. Production and staging are fully separated environments with distinct credentials.
Monitoring & loggingStructured logging with sensitive-value redaction. Security events — sign-ins, session revocations, report-share access — are recorded in dedicated audit trails, with metrics and traces collected through OpenTelemetry.
Secure developmentAutomated static analysis, secret, dependency, and infrastructure scanning runs on every change, with container images scanned before deployment. Test suites gate releases, and CI uses short-lived, keyless cloud credentials with least-privilege permissions.
Organizational securityProduction access is limited to authorized personnel under least-privilege roles. Staff handling of patient data is governed by our privacy and security policies, applicable regional obligations, and the agreements described above.
AI & data use

Clear rules for AI.

What AI is allowed to do with patient information — and what it is not.

No training

Your data does not train AI

n1 does not use Customer Patient Data to train general-purpose AI models, and does not permit AI providers to do so, unless the customer gives separate, express authorization.

Minimum data

Only what the task needs

The Service sends AI providers the minimum information needed for a task, under service-provider and subprocessor agreements.

Human review

A clinician decides

Qualified human review is required before an output is used in care. n1 supports documentation and preparation — it never makes the clinical decision and is not a diagnostic device.

Security FAQ

Questions about data protection.

Direct answers about compliance, encryption, access, retention, and sharing.

Ask a security question →
How is patient data encrypted?

Data is encrypted in transit with TLS 1.3 and at rest with AES-256. This covers traffic between the browser and n1, database connections, and stored source records and uploaded files.

Who can access patient records?

The clinician who uploads records and the patients they grant access to. Within n1, access to patient data is limited to authorized personnel under least-privilege roles, and access and modification are logged. AI providers receive only the minimum data needed for each processing task, under service-provider agreements, and never for training their models.

Is patient data used to train AI models?

No. n1 does not use Customer Patient Data to train general-purpose AI models, and does not permit an AI provider to use that data to train its general-purpose models, unless the customer gives separate, express authorization.

Read the AI and training section in our Privacy Notice →

Where is data stored — does it leave my country?

Records are stored encrypted on AWS infrastructure in the United States, certified to SOC 2 Type II. Where cross-border transfer rules apply, recognized safeguards such as standard contractual clauses are used, as described in the Privacy Notice. In-country hosting is not currently offered — contact us to discuss requirements for your jurisdiction.

Who owns the data, and how long is it kept?

Uploaded records remain your data — n1 processes them only to provide and secure the service, on your instructions. Information is retained only as long as needed to provide the service: patients can delete their account from settings, deletion requests are honored as described in the Privacy Notice, and data can be exported at any time as CSV, Excel, PDF, or Word. Separate legal requirements may still apply to account and audit data.

How is report sharing controlled?

A report becomes visible to the patient only when the clinician turns on patient access for it. Patients can also share a report by link — locked with a password, given an end date, tracked by view count, and revocable at any time.

Do I need patient consent before uploading records?

That depends on your jurisdiction and role: as the party providing the records, you are responsible for having the authority to do so — through consent, treatment relationship, or another lawful basis your rules recognize. n1 supports this with BAAs, a DPA, and processing strictly on your instructions.

Can I upload de-identified records?

Yes. What you upload is under your control — records can be de-identified before upload, and patient profiles can be created under a pseudonym without email or identity verification (a date of birth and sex are required for clinical context). Generated reports also anonymize identifiers in analysis sections: names, providers, and facilities are not referenced in clinical reasoning.

How do I verify AI-generated content and catch errors?

Supported extracted facts and report findings link back to the original source document and page where source metadata is available, so any statement can be checked against the record it came from. A report that misses the mark can be regenerated with revised instructions, and nothing reaches a patient until the clinician turns on access.

Does n1 integrate with our EMR?

Not yet for self-service accounts — current accounts use direct file upload (PDF for records; TXT/CSV for raw DNA data). Additional import and export options, including potential EHR/EMR integrations, are in development for enterprise deployments.

Can n1 run on-premises or self-hosted?

No — n1 is delivered as a managed cloud service. Enterprise onboarding documents the deployment's access controls, subprocessors, and incident-response process for institutional review.

Which privacy laws does n1 comply with in my country?

In the EEA, UK, and Switzerland, health and genetic data are treated as special-category data under GDPR, processed with a lawful basis and an additional condition, with cross-border transfer safeguards in place; a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) is available to customers. In the US, n1 supports HIPAA workflows and honors state privacy rights to access, correction, deletion, and portability — and never sells patient data or uses it for targeted advertising. For other countries, we work with customers to determine whether additional approvals or agreements are needed.

Is n1.care HIPAA compliant?

Yes, for deployments where HIPAA applies. n1.care supports HIPAA-compliant workflows and maintains Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) with service providers and partners that handle protected health information (PHI) on our behalf. Healthcare organizations can also enter into a BAA directly with n1.
