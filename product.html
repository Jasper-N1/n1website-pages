Product FAQ

Questions, answered.

Open a question for the short version. We can walk through the details with you.

Ask us in a walkthrough
What can I upload?

You can upload health records as PDFs and supported TXT, CSV, or TSV files. Typical records include laboratory and imaging reports, clinical notes, specialist letters, medication lists, and genetic reports supplied as readable documents. n1 extracts relevant facts such as results, dates, units, reference ranges, diagnoses, medications, supplements, and procedures, while retaining a link to the source. Raw genetic-data files are not supported.

What happens after I upload a file?

n1 first checks that the file can be processed, then reads the document and identifies its dates, document type, and clinically relevant content. It extracts structured information such as biomarker results and reference ranges, diagnoses, medications, supplements, procedures, and genetics findings when those details are present. The information is organized into the patient’s longitudinal record, with every extracted item linked back to the original file and page so you can verify it in context. The uploaded record remains available in the patient workspace for review; nothing is automatically approved or shared with the patient. You review the extracted data and any resulting report before deciding what to edit, approve, or share.

Can I check each result against the original file?

Yes. Each extracted item retains its connection to the source document and page, together with available context such as the result date, unit, and reference range. You can open that evidence while reviewing the patient record or a draft report, compare the extracted value with the original, and correct the structured record if needed. This traceability is designed to support clinical review rather than asking you to accept an isolated AI-generated statement.

Can I make a custom report?

Yes. You can begin with a built-in report type or describe the purpose and structure of the report you need. Select the intended reader, relevant records, units, level of detail, and any specialty or condition-specific focus before n1 prepares the draft. You can then revise the wording, add clinical context, remove sections, and approve only the version you are comfortable sharing.

Who approves the final report?

The clinician does. n1 prepares a draft, but it does not independently finalize or send clinical conclusions to a patient. The responsible clinician reviews the underlying sources, checks the extracted facts and recommendations, edits the wording where necessary, and explicitly approves the report before sharing it. Clinical judgment and responsibility remain with the treating professional.

How do patient invites work?

A clinician can create a private patient workspace first and invite the patient when access is appropriate; an invitation is optional and can be sent later. The patient receives a secure invitation to create or connect an account, and the connection becomes active only after acceptance. Patients can also request a connection with a clinician. Access remains governed by the workspace and report-sharing controls rather than exposing every record automatically.

How is patient data protected?

Patient files are encrypted in transit and at rest, and access is limited through authenticated accounts and workspace permissions. Available privacy controls can include hiding identifying details, processing without retaining the original source file, selecting an available storage region, and controlling which approved reports are shared. The appropriate configuration depends on your organization, location, and workflow. Review our current security and compliance information →

Can I try n1 before I sign up?

Yes. Open the live demo without signing up to explore a sample patient record and see how source-linked information and reports are presented. When you are ready to work with your own files, you can start a free trial. Practices evaluating workflow, governance, or team deployment can also book a walkthrough.
